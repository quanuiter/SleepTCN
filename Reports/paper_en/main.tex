\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Background.} Comparisons between deep sleep-staging models are frequently confounded, because
architecture, preprocessing, subject partitioning and model-selection rules change together across
publications. Reported gains therefore cannot be attributed to any single component, and out-of-domain
behaviour is rarely characterised beyond a single aggregate score.

\noindent
\textbf{Methods.} We conducted a pre-specified, subject-wise controlled comparison of six configurations
(E0--E4, E6) on the Sleep Cassette subset of Sleep-EDF Expanded (78 subjects, 153 recordings,
195{,}469 scored 30\,s epochs, Fpz--Cz). All configurations shared one 10-fold subject-wise partition,
one model-selection rule (validation \mf) and one locked test set that was opened exactly once.
Configurations differed in a single component at a time, isolating the sequence model (BiLSTM
$\rightarrow$ TCN), the epoch encoder (15-branch CNN $\rightarrow$ ResNet-1D) and the amplitude-handling
scheme. Uncertainty was quantified by paired subject-cluster bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples) and
two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm correction over four pre-specified comparisons.
Generalisation was assessed by locked zero-shot inference on 180 SHHS Visit-1 participants (C4--A1,
169{,}012 scored epochs); no weights were updated on SHHS.

\noindent
\textbf{Results.} The ResNet-1D--TCN configuration with fixed-scale amplitude handling (E3) attained the
highest out-of-fold \mf\ (0.7904). Against per-record $z$-scoring (E6) it gained $0.0213$
(\ci\ $[0.0122, 0.0307]$; Holm $p = 0.0012$); a post-protocol repeat with a second seed preserved the
direction ($+0.0102$, \ci\ $[0.0024, 0.0180]$) but not Holm significance. Isolated architectural
substitutions produced small, non-significant descriptive gains. E3 was $3.76\times$ faster at
inference than the CNN--BiLSTM control, at $4.37\times$ the parameter count and $28.4\%$ higher peak GPU
memory. Zero-shot on SHHS1, E3 exceeded the control by $0.0412$ subject-mean \mf\
(\ci\ $[0.0314, 0.0512]$; Holm $p = 2.65\times10^{-13}$) and $z$-scoring by $0.0274$.
Two novel analyses reframe the findings. First, the entire Sleep-EDF $\rightarrow$ SHHS1 degradation
($\Delta\mf = 0.1805$) is concentrated in two confusion channels: correcting N3$\rightarrow$N2 alone
recovers $0.1345$ ($74.5\%$ of the gap), and correcting N3$\rightarrow$N2 together with N2$\rightarrow$REM
recovers $0.1848$, restoring in-domain-level performance. The failure is a slow-wave amplitude-threshold
effect (N3 precision $0.944$, recall $0.258$, falling further to $0.073$ at stage boundaries) whose
direction is \emph{opposite} to the label-prior shift, which excludes prior shift as its cause and
identifies a montage- and age-dependent voltage criterion instead.
Second, the null result of the context ablation is explained
rather than merely reported: the non-causal TCN already integrates a receptive field of 253 epochs
($\pm63$\,min), so the $\pm1$-epoch context groups it was asked to supply are nested inside information
the sequence model already possesses.

\noindent
\textbf{Conclusions.} Within this protocol the complete E3 pipeline is the strongest and most
transfer-robust configuration, but its advantage is attributable to the evaluated procedure as a whole
rather than to architectural optimality. The dominant obstacle to deployment is not aggregate accuracy
but a localised, diagnosable and---we argue---correctable montage-dependent failure on slow-wave sleep.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sleep staging assigns one of five labels---W, N1, N2, N3 and REM---to every 30\,s epoch of a
polysomnographic recording. Manual scoring is slow, requires trained expertise, and retains
irreducible disagreement between scorers; in the inter-scorer reliability programme of the American
Academy of Sleep Medicine, N1 shows the lowest agreement of all stages~\cite{rosenberg2013aasm}. An
automated system therefore has to be judged not by overall accuracy alone, which is dominated by the
most frequent stages, but by class-balanced metrics, by stability across individuals, and by its
behaviour when the recording device, montage or population changes.

Progress in this field is difficult to read from published tables. Studies differ simultaneously in
dataset version, subject partitioning, wake-trimming policy, filtering, normalisation, sequence length
and model-selection rule, so a reported improvement cannot be attributed to the architecture that the
paper nominally proposes. What is missing is not another architecture but a protocol in which one
component changes at a time while everything else is held fixed.

We take ZleepAnlystNet~\cite{jadhav2024zleepanlystnet}, a two-stage single-channel model that couples a
bank of class-specific convolutional encoders to a BiLSTM, as an internal control, and we substitute two
components in isolation: a temporal convolutional network (TCN) as a parallelisable sequence
model~\cite{bai2018tcn}, and a one-dimensional residual network (ResNet-1D) as an epoch
encoder~\cite{he2016resnet}. Neither substitution is assumed beneficial. We additionally treat the
amplitude-handling scheme as a first-class experimental factor rather than an implementation detail,
because it determines whether absolute microvolt relationships survive preprocessing---the quantity most
likely to differ between acquisition setups.

The study is organised around four questions. (i) Does a TCN improve on a BiLSTM when the epoch encoder
is held fixed? (ii) Does a ResNet-1D encoder help when the sequence model is held fixed? (iii) How does
the amplitude-handling scheme affect performance? (iv) Do any of these effects survive zero-shot
evaluation on an independent, clinically distinct cohort?

Our contributions are the following.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item A leakage-controlled 10-fold subject-wise protocol in which six configurations share one partition,
one selection rule and one test set opened exactly once, with component substitutions designed so that
each pre-specified contrast isolates a single change.
\item A locked zero-shot transfer campaign to 180 SHHS Visit-1 participants, and a paired,
subject-level statistical treatment of it.
\item \emph{A decomposition of the domain gap.} We show that the cross-cohort degradation is not diffuse:
two confusion channels account for essentially all of it, and the dominant one is a slow-wave
amplitude-threshold failure whose signature (very high precision, collapsed recall) and whose direction
relative to the label-prior shift jointly identify it as a signal-level, montage-dependent effect rather
than a prior-shift or class-imbalance effect.
\item \emph{A mechanistic account of a null result.} We show that the context-group ablation could not
have detected an effect of the design that was used, because the sequence model's receptive field
already subsumes the context it was being asked to provide---turning an uninformative negative into a
constraint on how such ablations must be designed.
\item Distribution-free effect sizes and an exact sign test reported alongside every paired comparison,
so that the strength of evidence is separable from the sample size.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, we distinguish pre-specified comparisons, secondary evidence and post-hoc contrasts, and we
confine claims to the evidence class that supports them.

\section{Related work}

DeepSleepNet combined a dual-branch CNN with a BiLSTM to capture epoch morphology and temporal
dependence~\cite{supratak2017deepsleepnet}. AttnSleep introduced attention over multi-resolution
features~\cite{eldele2021attnsleep}, and SleepTransformer applied self-attention with explicit
uncertainty quantification~\cite{phan2022sleeptransformer}. Together these establish that sequential
context matters substantially. They also illustrate the comparability problem: differences in dataset
version, subject split and preprocessing between these works are large enough that cross-table
comparison cannot substitute for an ablation carried out inside one protocol, a point emphasised in
recent reviews of the field~\cite{phan2022review}.

Sleep-EDF Expanded is the standard public benchmark for single-channel
EEG~\cite{kemp2000sleepEDF,physionetSleepEDF}. The Sleep Heart Health Study (SHHS) is a large
multi-centre cohort assembled to study sleep-disordered breathing and cardiovascular
risk~\cite{quan1997shhs}, distributed through the National Sleep Research Resource
(NSRR)~\cite{zhang2018nsrr}. The two differ in age, comorbidity, recording hardware, montage and label
distribution. This makes SHHS a considerably more realistic transfer test than a held-out split of the
same corpus, and it is the reason we treat the transfer result---not the in-domain ranking---as the
principal evidence about deployability.

Two threads of the literature bear directly on our findings. First, the sleep slow oscillation has a
pronounced anterior amplitude maximum and propagates as a travelling wave~\cite{massimini2004travelling},
so the microvolt amplitude of slow-wave activity is derivation-dependent; and slow-wave amplitude
declines markedly with age~\cite{ohayon2004metaanalysis}. Both scoring
manuals~\cite{rechtschaffen1968,berry2017aasm} define deep sleep partly through an absolute amplitude
criterion, which means an amplitude criterion learned on one derivation and one age band need not
transfer. Second, classifier outputs trained under one label prior are miscalibrated under another, and
the correction is well characterised~\cite{saerens2002adjusting,guo2017calibration}. Section~\ref{sec:gap}
uses these two mechanisms as competing explanations and shows that the data discriminate between them.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Data and labels}

The Sleep Cassette subset of Sleep-EDF Expanded comprises 153 recordings from 78 subjects; we use the
Fpz--Cz derivation at 100\,Hz with 30\,s epochs (3{,}000 samples per epoch). Annotations are mapped to
W, N1, N2, N3 and REM; Movement and Unknown epochs retain their temporal position but are excluded from
the loss and from all metrics. After windowing, 195{,}767 epochs remain, of which 195{,}469 are scored
and 298 are Movement/Unknown.

An evaluation window is applied identically to both cohorts. It is anchored on the first and last true
sleep epoch (any of N1, N2, N3, REM) and extended by 30\,min of wake on each side, i.e.\ 60 epochs per
side. This policy is stated explicitly because it strongly affects the wake fraction and hence every
aggregate metric, and because it is one of the principal reasons published numbers are not directly
comparable. It is a benchmark window, not a deployable inference rule, since its anchors use ground-truth
labels.

The SHHS Visit-1 sample was locked before any prediction was inspected and comprises 180 test
participants, with a further 5 adaptation and 15 validation participants that were never used to update
weights. The EEG channel is C4--A1 at 125\,Hz, resampled to 100\,Hz with a polyphase anti-aliasing
filter (up 4, down 5, Kaiser window $\beta = 5.0$) applied to the continuous signal before epoching.
SHHS labels are mapped $0\rightarrow$W, $1\rightarrow$N1, $2\rightarrow$N2, $\{3,4\}\rightarrow$N3,
$5\rightarrow$REM, $\{6,9\}\rightarrow$excluded. Table~\ref{tab:class-distribution} reports both label
distributions.

\subsection{Preprocessing conditions}

All conditions operate on the continuous calibrated signal in microvolts before epoching. Four
conditions enter the comparison:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Raw} (E0--E2): no filtering, no clipping, no rescaling; native $\mu$V retained.
\item \textbf{Band-pass only} (E4): zero-phase Butterworth band-pass, order 4, 0.5--30\,Hz, implemented
as second-order sections with forward--backward filtering.
\item \textbf{Fixed-scale amplitude handling} (E3): the same band-pass, followed by a hard clip at
$\pm800\,\mu$V and division by a constant factor of 100. The clip threshold is an absolute constant
identical for every recording, so no data-dependent statistic is estimated and no leakage is possible;
crucially, \emph{relative amplitude between recordings is preserved}.
\item \textbf{Per-record $z$-score} (E6): the same band-pass and clip, followed by subtraction of the
mean and division by the standard deviation computed over the whole filtered, clipped recording. This
is label-independent but \emph{destroys absolute amplitude information across recordings}.
\end{itemize}

No notch filter is applied: 50\,Hz lies outside the 0.5--30\,Hz pass-band and at the Nyquist frequency
of the 100\,Hz signal. The contrast E3\,--\,E6 is thus precisely a contrast between preserving and
discarding cross-recording amplitude scale, which is why it is the pre-specified comparison of greatest
interest for transfer. One further derived variant proved bit-identical to the band-pass-only condition
in this corpus and therefore does not constitute an independent experiment.

Table~\ref{tab:configs} lists the six configurations.

\paragraph{15-branch CNN encoder.} The control encoder instantiates 15 independent small CNNs: three
context groups---C (current epoch), P (previous), N (next)---crossed with five class-specific detectors
(W, N1, N2, N3, REM). Each branch has 2{,}495 parameters and comprises two convolutional blocks,
\texttt{Conv1d}$(1\!\rightarrow\!10, k{=}55)$ and \texttt{Conv1d}$(10\!\rightarrow\!5, k{=}25)$, each
followed by ReLU, batch normalisation and $16\times$ max-pooling, then a $60\rightarrow10\rightarrow5$
head. The concatenated output is a 75-dimensional vector per epoch (15 branches $\times$ 5 softmax
outputs), ordered C, P, N. Context shifts are clamped at recording boundaries.

\paragraph{ResNet-1D encoder.} A stem \texttt{Conv1d}$(1\!\rightarrow\!32, k{=}50, s{=}5)$ with batch
normalisation, ReLU and max-pooling is followed by three residual blocks
($32\rightarrow64$, $s{=}1$; $64\rightarrow128$, $s{=}2$; $128\rightarrow128$, $s{=}2$), each containing
two $k{=}7$ convolutions with a projection shortcut where shape changes. Global average pooling yields a
128-dimensional epoch embedding; total temporal downsampling is $40\times$. Note that the ResNet-1D
encoder consumes \emph{only the current epoch}: it has no C/P/N construction.

\paragraph{Sequence models.} The TCN projects the encoder output to 128 dimensions and applies six
residual blocks of two $k{=}3$ convolutions each, with dilations $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32$, LayerNorm, GELU
and dropout $0.2$. Padding is symmetric, so the network is \emph{non-causal}, and the receptive field is
$1 + 2(k-1)\sum_i d_i = 1 + 4 \times 63 = 253$ epochs, i.e.\ approximately $\pm126$ epochs or
$\pm63$\,min around the epoch being classified. The BiLSTM control is a single bidirectional layer with
128 hidden units per direction. In both cases the input is the \emph{entire overnight recording} as one
sequence (typically 900--1{,}200 epochs), not a short window; padding is masked at every layer. The
receptive-field figure is central to interpreting the context ablation and we return to it in
Section~\ref{sec:rf}.

\subsection{Two-stage training and subject-wise cross-validation}

The 78 subjects are partitioned into 10 subject-disjoint folds. In each evaluation round one fold is the
test fold, the next fold (modulo 10) is the validation fold and the remainder are training folds. Both
nights of a subject always share the same role. The same partition is used for every configuration, and
the test fold never participates in model selection; the training API accepts only training and
validation loaders, so test data cannot enter fitting.

Training proceeds in two stages with a \emph{fully frozen} encoder. Stage 1 fits the epoch encoder at
epoch level: for E0/E1, the 15 CNN branches are trained independently as five-class problems under
class-specific binary weights $w_c = 0.5 N / n_c$ and $w_{j \neq c} = 0.5 N /(N - n_c)$ computed from
training subjects only; for E2--E6, the ResNet-1D and a linear head are trained on single epochs under
balanced weights $w_c = N/(5 n_c)$, again from training subjects only. The best checkpoint is then
verified by SHA-256, switched to evaluation mode, and used under \texttt{torch.no\_grad()} to extract and
cache features. Stage 2 fits the sequence model on those cached features; because the inputs are arrays,
the encoder is not in the computation graph and the freeze is absolute.

Optimisation settings are: 15-branch CNN, Adam, lr $10^{-3}$, batch 64 epochs, patience 10; ResNet-1D,
AdamW, lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 64, max 40 epochs, patience 8; BiLSTM, Adam,
lr $10^{-2}$, batch 4 recordings, patience 10; TCN, Adam, lr $5\times10^{-4}$, batch 8 recordings,
max 300 epochs, patience 30. Gradient-norm clipping at 1.0 is applied to sequence models. There is no
learning-rate schedule, no label smoothing and no data augmentation. Sequence models use unweighted
masked cross-entropy, so their outputs are calibrated to the Sleep-EDF label prior---a fact that
Section~\ref{sec:gap} exploits. Model selection uses validation \mf\ (validation weighted loss for the
CNN branches). E1 reuses the E0 encoder checkpoints unchanged, so E1\,--\,E0 isolates the sequence-model
substitution exactly.

\subsection{Test locking, statistical analysis and effect sizes}

All six configurations completed validation-based selection before the test set was opened, once. In
Sleep-EDF, pooled \mf\ is the descriptive headline metric, but the unit of statistical inference is the
subject. Paired subject-cluster bootstrap with 10{,}000 resamples estimates confidence intervals for the
difference in pooled \mf, while a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945} assesses the
subject-level distribution of per-subject differences~\cite{efron1994bootstrap}. Holm
correction~\cite{holm1979} is applied over the four pre-specified comparisons E1\,--\,E0, E2\,--\,E1,
E3\,--\,E2 and E3\,--\,E6. These two procedures target different estimands---a pooled, epoch-weighted
quantity and a subject-averaged rank quantity---and may therefore disagree; where they do, we say so
explicitly rather than reporting only the favourable one. E3\,--\,E0 is labelled post-hoc throughout.

To make the strength of evidence separable from sample size we additionally report, for every paired
comparison, a distribution-free effect size derived from the signs of the paired differences,
\begin{equation}
\dsign \;=\; \frac{W - L}{W + L},
\end{equation}
where $W$ and $L$ are the numbers of subjects improved and degraded. This is Cliff's
dominance statistic~\cite{cliff1993dominance} evaluated on the sign of the paired difference; it equals
$2\hat{p}_{\mathrm{win}} - 1$ and lies in $[-1, 1]$. We accompany it with the exact two-sided binomial
sign test. These statistics use less information than the Wilcoxon test---they ignore magnitudes---so
they are reported as unadjusted supplementary robustness statistics, not as members of the
pre-specified family. Their value is that they are invariant to the metric's scale and immune to a few
large-magnitude subjects.

After the primary results had been observed, the whole campaign was repeated with a second fixed
training seed on the same partition. The two seeds are analysed separately; $p$-values are never pooled,
and two fixed seeds are not treated as a sample from the distribution over initialisations. This repeat
is reported as a post-protocol sensitivity analysis.

For SHHS, fold predictions are combined at subject level. The pre-locked primary criterion is
subject-mean \mf, with E3\,--\,E0 and E3\,--\,E6 as the two locked comparisons under the same
bootstrap and Wilcoxon--Holm treatment. The E1/E2 component analysis is secondary evidence on an
already-opened sample, and E3\,--\,E2 is explicitly post-hoc.

\subsection{Supporting evaluations}

Inference timing was measured on identical hardware (Tesla V100) for all configurations and excludes
I/O, preprocessing and training. Measurements comprise three rounds of 100 forward passes, and the
configuration order was shuffled independently in each round to remove ordering and thermal bias; we
report the median across rounds. Feature spaces were compared by silhouette
coefficient~\cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes} on a stratified subsample of 200 epochs per class per fold
(1{,}000 epochs per fold, 10{,}000 in total) after reduction to 20 principal components; this subsampling
is a limitation of that analysis and we treat it as descriptive only. The
context-group ablation retains the sequence model unchanged and replaces the ablated group with its
per-dimension mean estimated on valid training epochs, preserving input dimensionality; its primary
criterion is \mf\ restricted to a transition neighbourhood of radius $r = 1$ epoch, with $r = 2$ as a
sensitivity check, and 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples.

\subsection{Feature geometry}

Across all ten folds without exception, the ResNet-1D embedding has a lower silhouette coefficient than
the 15-branch CNN feature vector: mean $0.1048$ (SD $0.0229$) versus $0.2126$ (SD $0.0356$), mean
difference $-0.1079$ (SD $0.0228$; range $-0.1526$ to $-0.0608$). The consistency is total (10/10 folds; Figure~\ref{fig:supporting}a),
so the effect is not noise.

The correct reading is not that the ResNet embedding is worse. Silhouette rewards compact, Euclidean,
per-epoch clusters, whereas the downstream TCN integrates a $\pm126$-epoch neighbourhood and can exploit
distributional or temporal structure that no per-epoch clustering index measures. The 15-branch CNN's
features are, by construction, 75 calibrated class posteriors---a representation almost engineered to
score well on silhouette---while the ResNet's 128 dimensions are unconstrained. That E2 nonetheless
matches or exceeds E1 downstream (Table~\ref{tab:sleepedf-performance}) is direct evidence that
per-epoch cluster separability is a poor proxy for sequence-level utility, and we report the silhouette
result mainly to document that negative finding.

Three descriptive observations deserve recording, with the explicit caveat that none is statistically
supported.

First, the effect of P and N is \emph{asymmetric}: adding the next epoch to the current one raises
overall \mf\ by $0.0041$, whereas adding the previous epoch raises it by $0.0003$---an order of magnitude
less. CN is in fact the descriptively best condition overall, exceeding the full CPN condition, and
FULL\,--\,CN is negative on overall \mf\ ($-0.0014$) and on accuracy ($-0.0011$).

the transition region, i.e.\ it changes sign between the two criteria. Whatever P and N contribute, they
do not contribute independently, and no single scalar summarises their joint effect. This is the
quantitative reason we regard any statement of the form ``group X carries $k\%$ of the information'' as
unsupportable here, and we recommend that the earlier gloss of this kind be withdrawn.

alone, full context helps at W$\leftrightarrow$N1 and N1$\leftrightarrow$REM but hurts at
N1$\leftrightarrow$N2 and N2$\leftrightarrow$N3, and the best condition differs by pair. A forward
asymmetry would be physiologically coherent, since several scoring rules are prospectively
defined---N2 onset is fixed by the first K-complex or spindle, and REM continuation rules look
ahead~\cite{berry2017aasm}---but all these differences are far smaller than their confidence intervals
and we advance them only as hypotheses for a study designed to test them.

A seed-123 extension evaluating E4 (band-pass only) reinforces this. E4 reached subject-mean \mf\

same-seed E2 by $0.0323$ and same-seed E3 by $0.0098$. Because E3 and E4 differ only by clipping and a
constant rescaling, and both preserve cross-recording amplitude relationships, this small difference
does not overturn the amplitude account developed below; it does indicate that the clip-and-rescale step
carries no transfer benefit over band-pass filtering alone, and that band-pass filtering is doing most
of the work. These are paired comparisons on an already-opened sample; they are neither equivalence nor
non-inferiority tests.

identified from the data already collected.

Three facts in Table~\ref{tab:transfer-perclass} constrain the explanation.

\paragraph{(i) The degradation is dominated by one class.} N3's F1 falls by $0.4030$; the other four
classes fall by $0.09$--$0.18$. N3 alone contributes $45\%$ of the total macro-averaged drop.

\paragraph{(ii) The N3 failure has a threshold signature.} N3 precision is \emph{higher} out of domain
than in ($0.944$ vs $0.807$) while recall collapses ($0.258$ vs $0.810$). The model emits N3 only
$0.273\times$ as often as it should, and $16{,}674$ of $22{,}806$ true N3 epochs---$73.1\%$---are
labelled N2. A model that is nearly always right when it fires but fires far too rarely is behaving like
a detector whose decision threshold sits too high for the incoming signal. It is not confused about
what N3 looks like; it is not seeing enough of it.

\paragraph{(iii) The miscalibration runs opposite to the label-prior shift.} This is the decisive
observation. SHHS1 contains $2.02\times$ as much N3 as Sleep-EDF, yet the model predicts N3 at
$0.273\times$ the required rate. It contains $0.38\times$ as much N1, yet the model over-predicts N1 by
$2.151\times$. If the failure were driven by prior shift---the standard explanation for
transfer degradation in an unweighted classifier---the errors would run \emph{with} the prior change,
and the standard correction~\cite{saerens2002adjusting} would repair them. Here that correction would
make N3 strictly worse. Prior shift is therefore excluded as the primary cause, and the cause must lie
at the signal level.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Principal findings}

Within the primary campaign, E3 exceeded E6 by $0.0213$ \mf\ with a fully positive bootstrap interval and
Holm significance, and the post-protocol repeat preserved a positive effect of $0.0102$. Across two runs
the evidence favours an amplitude-handling scheme that preserves relationships between recordings over
one that normalises each recording independently, while the shifting Holm-adjusted $p$-value shows the
inferential strength remains contingent on randomisation. The same ordering held out of domain.

The complete E3 pipeline outperformed the E0 control in both cohorts. On SHHS1 this was one of two
comparisons locked before the test set was opened. Because E3 alters architecture and preprocessing
together, the supportable conclusion is that E3 as an evaluated procedure transfers better within this
protocol; credit for individual components must come from the component contrasts, and those tell a more
complicated story.

\subsection{Why the context ablation was null: receptive-field redundancy}
\label{sec:rf}

The context ablation found no added value from the P and N feature groups
(Table~\ref{tab:context-comparisons}). Reported as a bare negative, this invites the wrong inference,
namely that neighbouring epochs carry little information about stage transitions---a conclusion in
tension with essentially the whole sequence-modelling literature~\cite{supratak2017deepsleepnet,
eldele2021attnsleep,phan2022sleeptransformer}.

The architecture resolves the tension. The C/P/N groups supply $\pm1$ epoch of context at the
\emph{encoder}. Downstream, the non-causal TCN operates over the entire overnight recording with a
receptive field of 253 epochs, i.e.\ $\pm126$ epochs or roughly $\pm63$\,min. The information that the P
and N branches provide is thus a strict subset of what the sequence model already has direct access to,
several times over. The ablation was not testing whether temporal context helps; it was testing whether
supplying a $\pm1$-epoch summary \emph{twice} helps. A null result is the expected outcome, and the
observed effect sizes---$|\Delta| < 0.0024$ on the transition criterion, intervals spanning zero---are
exactly the size one would predict from redundant inputs.

This reframing has three consequences. It explains why the FULL condition can lose to CN
(Table~\ref{tab:context-ablation}): redundant inputs add parameters and noise without adding
information. It means the result licenses no conclusion whatsoever about the value of temporal context
in sleep staging; it constrains only the marginal value of encoder-level context \emph{given} a
long-receptive-field sequence model. And it prescribes the design a genuine test would require: ablate
the TCN's receptive field itself---by restricting dilations, by making the network causal, or by
windowing the input---rather than ablating features nested inside it. The sign-changing interaction
index reported in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} independently confirms that no scalar summary of the
groups' contribution is available from this design.

We note in passing that the descriptive asymmetry favouring the next epoch over the previous one
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) survives this reframing as a hypothesis worth testing under a properly
context-limited design, since prospective scoring rules give it an independent physiological rationale.

\subsection{An amplitude-threshold account of the N3 collapse}

Section~\ref{sec:gap} established that the transfer failure is localised in N3, has a
high-precision/low-recall threshold signature, and runs counter to the label-prior shift. We propose the
following account, which is consistent with every observation and makes falsifiable predictions.

Both scoring manuals define deep sleep partly through an absolute voltage criterion---slow waves
exceeding roughly $75\,\mu$V peak-to-peak occupying a minimum fraction of the
epoch~\cite{rechtschaffen1968,berry2017aasm}. A model trained on Sleep-EDF learns this criterion
implicitly, in the units and at the derivation it was trained on: Fpz--Cz, in a corpus of comparatively
healthy subjects. Two things change in SHHS1. First, the derivation is C4--A1. The sleep slow
oscillation has a pronounced frontal amplitude maximum and propagates
antero-posteriorly~\cite{massimini2004travelling}, so identical slow-wave activity registers at
substantially lower amplitude at a central-mastoid derivation than at a frontopolar one. Second, SHHS
participants are markedly older, and slow-wave amplitude declines strongly with
age~\cite{ohayon2004metaanalysis}. Both effects push genuine N3 epochs below the learned amplitude
criterion, in the same direction.

The account makes four predictions, and all four are borne out by data already collected.
(i) N3 recall should collapse while N3 precision stays high or rises, because the epochs that still
clear the threshold are unambiguously N3---observed, precision $0.944$ against recall $0.258$.
(ii) The misplaced epochs should go overwhelmingly to N2, the adjacent stage differing chiefly in
slow-wave quantity---observed, $73.1\%$ of all true N3.
(iii) The effect should be insensitive to filtering, since filtering does not restore amplitude that the
derivation and the ageing cortex never delivered---observed, N3 recall is $0.2582$ for E3 and $0.2496$
for E2 despite markedly different preprocessing, the only class where E3's large preprocessing advantage
essentially vanishes.
(iv) The failure should be graded by proximity to the amplitude criterion, hence worst at the edges of
N3 bouts where slow-wave activity is least established---observed, and strikingly: N3 recall falls
further to $0.0733$ in the transition neighbourhood (Table~\ref{tab:shhs-transition}), with $90.6\%$ of
transition-region N3 going to N2, while precision there remains $0.831$.

Prediction (iv) is the one that discriminates most sharply. A model that had simply failed to learn a
transferable concept of N3 would degrade roughly uniformly across N3 epochs. A model applying a
miscalibrated amplitude threshold must fail most where the true signal sits closest to that threshold.
The observed three-and-a-half-fold gradient between whole-sample and transition-region recall is the
signature of the latter.

There is an uncomfortable corollary. The design choice that makes E3 the best configuration overall---
preserving absolute amplitude relationships rather than $z$-scoring each recording---is the same choice
that ties the model's N3 criterion to an absolute voltage scale and thus makes it montage-dependent.
E3's principal strength and its worst per-class failure share a cause. The evaluation is currently blind
to this trade-off because macro-F1 averages the N3 collapse against gains elsewhere.

This yields a decisive and cheap experiment that we did not run and now recommend. Per-record
$z$-scoring (E6) removes absolute scale and re-expresses amplitude relative to each recording's own
distribution, which is the quantity a montage change most nearly preserves. The account predicts that E6
should show \emph{substantially better N3 recall on SHHS1 than E3}, even though E6 is worse overall
($0.5407$ vs $0.5680$ subject-mean \mf). Per-class SHHS metrics for E6 were not computed in this
campaign; obtaining them requires no retraining, only re-scoring existing predictions, and would either
confirm the amplitude account or refute it outright. If confirmed, the practical implication is not to
adopt E6 wholesale but to decouple the two roles---absolute scale for stages where it helps, relative
scale for the N3 criterion---or to recalibrate the N3 decision threshold on a small target-domain
sample. The 5 adaptation and 15 validation SHHS participants held out for exactly this purpose and never
used remain available.

\subsection{The roles of the TCN and the ResNet-1D encoder}

In-domain, E1\,--\,E0 and E2\,--\,E1 gave positive descriptive gains that did not survive Holm
correction. Out of domain, E1\,--\,E0 stayed positive but small while E2\,--\,E1 turned significantly
negative. The architectural benefit is therefore domain-dependent, and the claim that ResNet-1D is
uniformly superior to the control encoder is not supported---it is contradicted out of domain on
unfiltered signals. The dependable advantage of ResNet-1D--TCN is operational: competitive accuracy with
a much simpler pipeline and $3.76\times$ faster inference, paid for with $4.37\times$ the parameters and
$28.4\%$ more memory. As Section~4.4 notes, that speed-up comes from replacing 15 sequential CNN
branches, not from the TCN.

The silhouette result is a useful negative: a widely used representation-quality index ranked the two
encoders opposite to their downstream utility, in 10 folds out of 10. Per-epoch Euclidean cluster
compactness is simply not the property a long-context sequence model needs, and we would caution against
using such indices to select encoders for sequential tasks.

\subsection{Preprocessing dominates transfer}

The largest effect in the study is E3\,--\,E2 on SHHS1 ($+0.0475$, $\dsign = +0.633$), a contrast in
which architecture is held fixed and only preprocessing changes. It is roughly seven times the largest
in-domain architectural contrast. The E4 extension shows band-pass filtering alone captures most of this,
with clipping and constant rescaling adding nothing for transfer. Since the largest architectural effect
we measured out of domain was \emph{negative}, the ordering is unambiguous within this study:
preprocessing choices dominated architecture choices for cross-cohort generalisation. Given how much
more attention architecture receives in this literature, we regard this as the finding with the widest
implications, while noting that E3 changes several operations at once and no single operation is
identified as causal.

\subsection{Positioning relative to published results}

We deliberately do not present a table of accuracies from other papers alongside ours. The wake-trimming
policy alone (Section~3.1) moves the wake fraction, and hence accuracy and macro-F1, by an amount
comparable to every architectural effect measured here; subject-count, split scheme, and channel choice
compound this. Published Sleep-EDF figures for the models discussed in
Section~2~\cite{supratak2017deepsleepnet,eldele2021attnsleep,phan2022sleeptransformer,
jadhav2024zleepanlystnet} were obtained under protocols that differ in several of these dimensions
simultaneously, so a numerical comparison would misrepresent the evidence in either direction. Our E0
control is a re-implementation of the ZleepAnlystNet design evaluated under \emph{our} protocol, and it
is the appropriate reference point for every number in this paper. Readers wishing to situate these
results externally should compare protocols first, and we have specified ours completely for that
purpose.

\subsection{Limitations and a prioritised agenda}

Results should be read within the data and protocol evaluated. SHHS1 is one specific out-of-domain
sample, not a population. The second seed corroborates direction but two fixed seeds cannot characterise
initialisation variance; a proper treatment would require enough seeds to estimate a distribution, and
we report no such claim. Several component analyses were run after the sample was opened and are
supporting evidence only. Single-channel EEG limits separability for N1 and REM in principle, and the
evaluation window uses ground-truth anchors, so it is an offline benchmarking device rather than a
deployable rule. We also report no calibration analysis: the sequence models are trained with unweighted
cross-entropy and their probability estimates are untested, which matters for any clinical use where an
abstain option would be valuable~\cite{guo2017calibration,phan2022sleeptransformer}.

Two reporting gaps should be stated plainly. Absolute-performance intervals are available for the SHHS
configurations (Table~\ref{tab:shhs-performance}) but not for the Sleep-EDF ones, where only
difference intervals were retained. And although paired win/tie/loss counts and median subject-level
differences were preserved for every comparison, the full per-subject \mf\ distributions were not
archived, so we cannot report the dispersion (IQR, range) of individual performance or identify which
participants the models handle worst. For a metric whose clinical relevance is inherently per-patient
this is a real limitation, and we would archive those vectors in any future campaign. Finally,
per-class SHHS metrics exist only for E2 and E3; obtaining them for E0, E1 and E6 requires no
retraining and would settle several questions raised above, including the decisive one in
Section~5.3.

Ordered by expected value, we would prioritise: (1) computing per-class SHHS metrics for E6 to test the
amplitude account, which requires no retraining; (2) recalibrating the N3 threshold on the held-out
SHHS adaptation participants, since $74.5\%$ of the domain gap sits in that single channel; (3) ablating
the TCN receptive field directly, which is the experiment the context ablation was unable to perform;
(4) estimating initialisation variance with a sufficient number of seeds; and (5) reporting calibration
and selective-prediction behaviour. Items (1) and (2) are inexpensive and address the largest single
source of error identified in this study.

\section{Conclusion}

A unified subject-wise design with a single locked test set allowed six configurations to be compared
fairly on Sleep-EDF. In the primary campaign E3 attained a descriptive \mf\ of $0.7904$ and exceeded E6
with a fully positive interval and Holm significance; a post-protocol repeat preserved the direction of
that effect. Isolated substitutions of the sequence model and the epoch encoder produced descriptive or
domain-dependent gains, but no stable advantage survived multiple-comparison correction. ResNet-1D--TCN
simplifies the pipeline and is $3.76\times$ faster at inference, at the cost of more parameters and
memory.

On SHHS1, E3 outperformed both locked references. The largest effect anywhere in the study, however, was
a preprocessing contrast under fixed architecture, and the largest architectural effect out of domain
was negative. Within this protocol, preprocessing---not architecture---governed cross-cohort transfer.

The two analyses we regard as most consequential are corrective rather than confirmatory. The
cross-cohort gap is not diffuse degradation: two confusion channels account for essentially all of it,
and the dominant one is a slow-wave amplitude-threshold failure that is diagnosable from its
precision--recall signature, that runs opposite to the label-prior shift, and that is plausibly
correctable without retraining. And the null result of the context ablation was structurally
predetermined by a sequence model whose receptive field already subsumed the context under test, so it
constrains experimental design rather than physiology. The contribution of this work is therefore a
protocol with out-of-domain evidence, a quantified operational trade-off, and a demonstration that
careful decomposition of where a model fails is more informative than the aggregate score that
summarises it.
